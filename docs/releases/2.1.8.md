# Pulse 2.1.8

Pulse 2.1.8 turns Trends into a topic map and brings together everything prepared for 2.1.7, which was never published: cluster outlines on the map, systematic screening with PRISMA 2020, evidence consensus, lighter loading for large libraries and bioRxiv/medRxiv lookup.

### Trends topic map
- **Every paper in one topic group.** Trends groups the whole library by the concepts the map already compares. Each group is a bubble with its papers in orbit, and newer papers sit on inner orbits. Groups are named after their most distinctive keywords, and papers without enough text to compare get a group of their own. Choose 6, 10, 15 or 20 groups.
- **One selection for the whole page.** Clicking a bubble, a paper dot, a year bar or a row in the list sets the same group, concept and period filters, shown as removable chips. The list opens from groups to their concepts to their papers, with Details, Open paper and View in graph for each paper. Selecting a concept lights up its papers on the map, including in other groups. Clicking a paper on the map opens its details.
- **The year chart is the timeline.** Bars still show exact counts and filter by period. Play steps through the bars while the map grows to each one.
- **Honest trends.** A change in share between the two latest three-year windows is coloured as rising or falling only when it is clear at 95%. Smaller changes are marked with “?”, for groups and concepts alike.
- The map and list sit side by side on wide windows and stack on narrow ones. The list carries everything the map shows, as text, and orbits stay still when the system asks for reduced motion.

### Map and graph
- **Cluster outlines.** The map draws soft outlines around research clusters, labelled with each cluster's most common keyword.
- **Citation cascades.** The timeline player animates how citations spread outward from earlier papers year by year.
- **Bridge papers.** Betweenness centrality finds the papers that connect otherwise separate clusters; one click highlights a bridge paper on the map.
- **SVG export** of the active cluster or filtered map with a colour legend.

### Systematic screening
- A live **PRISMA 2020** flow diagram, from identification to inclusion with exclusion reasons, with vector download.
- **Active-learning order:** candidates most likely to be included move up as you record decisions.
- **Dual-reviewer blind mode:** export anonymised projects for a second reviewer and reconcile disagreements, with Cohen's κ.

### Evidence
- Full-text harvesting targets Methods, Results, Discussion and Limitations sections.
- Findings about the same target or intervention are grouped and marked as agreeing or conflicting.
- Clicking a supporting quote opens the PDF at its page. Tables in PDFs are read row by row.

### Libraries and sources
- **bioRxiv and medRxiv:** preprint DOIs get their details, links to the published version, and open PDFs where the server allows it.
- **Lighter loading:** large libraries load paper metadata first and fetch full content when needed.
- **Single-file backups:** a portable `.pulse-workspace` archive holds the library, screening projects, saved searches and local PDFs, and can be inspected before restoring.
- A **What's New** screen appears once after each update and stays available in Settings.
- Scripts and stylesheets are versioned per release, so an update never runs a mix of cached old and new code.

Mac and Windows packages use the same 2.1.8 source and include Python, PDF/OCR tools and the offline search ranker. Existing Pulse 2.1.2 or later installations can use **Settings → App updates**. Update packages carry signatures verified by Pulse. The Mac app remains ad hoc signed and is not Apple-notarized; the Windows installer has no publisher certificate.
