# Pulse 1.6.0

11 October 2026

Mac installer: [Pulse-1.6.0.dmg](https://github.com/7drbw7mdgf-lgtm/Pulse/releases/download/v1.6.0/Pulse-1.6.0.dmg) (Apple Silicon, ad hoc signed).

Pulse 1.6 makes paper identification much more reliable. It no longer attaches the wrong paper to a title that merely looks similar, and it cleans up duplicate and junk records in discovery results.

## Paper identification

- **No more confident wrong matches.** Titles are now compared word by word, in order. A title that differs by one meaningful word ("…in mice" versus "…in rats") or only shares its words in a different order ("Is attention all you need?") is no longer accepted as the same paper.
- **Title alone must be near-exact.** A partial or garbled title is accepted only when two independent fields (authors, year, journal or pages) also agree. A missing subtitle, a typo in a long word, typographic dashes and accents still match.
- **Wider candidate search.** Crossref now returns 10 candidates, and OpenAlex is always consulted unless Crossref already found an exact title. Previously a famous paper could be missed entirely when review records filled Crossref's first five results.
- **Review and correction records are ignored.** Faculty Opinions/F1000 recommendations, peer-review reports, decision letters, corrections, errata and retraction notices no longer compete with the paper they describe.
- **Pasted references resolve.** Numbered (Vancouver) references such as `Huang C, Wang Y, et al. Title. Lancet 2020;395(10223):497-506.` are parsed into authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue and pages. In any citation style, a candidate whose full title appears inside the pasted reference is recognised.
- **Same-title ties are resolved carefully.** A published article is preferred over its own preprint (shared authors required), and a decisively more-cited work wins an exact-title tie. Otherwise the result is reported as ambiguous rather than guessed.

Live check against Crossref and OpenAlex on 10 exact titles of well-known papers: 1.5.9 identified 5 correctly, attached the wrong paper to 2 and missed 3. 1.6.0 identified all 10 papers, attached no wrong papers, and resolved a messy pasted citation and a misspelled title that 1.5.9 could not.

## Discovery results

- **Repository copies fold into the published record.** Copies of a paper on Zenodo, figshare, institutional repositories, Research Square, Authorea and preprint servers, and copies without a DOI, are grouped under the publisher's DOI and listed as related versions.
- **Supplementary-file DOIs** (for example `….s001` or PLOS figure DOIs) map to the parent paper.
- **Impossible years** from index artefacts (such as 1753 for a 2023 paper) are blanked instead of shown.
- **Generic titles no longer flood results.** Repeats of short generic titles ("Protein structure prediction") move to the end of the list instead of filling the first pages.
- Distinct publisher editions and numbered series ("Part 1", "Part 2") stay separate.

In a live AlphaFold discovery run, duplicate entries in the top 200 fell from about 45 to 2 (two genuinely separate protocol editions).

## Checks

129 backend tests (18 new for identification and duplicates), 23 root workspace tests and 15 JavaScript suites passed. The synthetic ranking benchmark is unchanged (Recall@50 1.0, nDCG@10 0.987); these changes affect identity and duplicates, not relevance scores.

Ranking still depends on provider availability: without a Semantic Scholar API key, rate limits (HTTP 429) can lower the position of strongly related papers in some runs.

Apple Silicon, macOS 11 or later, and Python 3.10 or later are required. PDF extraction dependencies are included; Python, Ollama and model weights are separate installations. The app is ad hoc signed and is not notarized.
