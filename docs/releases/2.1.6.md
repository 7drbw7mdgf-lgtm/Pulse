# Pulse 2.1.6

The PDF reader scrolls continuously through the whole paper. Pages near the screen are rendered at the current zoom and the screen's resolution, so text stays sharp on Retina displays, and distant pages are not loaded until needed. Zoom offers Fit width, Fit page and 50–300%, the − and + buttons, Cmd/Ctrl with +, − or 0, and Ctrl/Cmd+scroll or a trackpad pinch. Arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Space and Home/End move through the paper, and each PDF reopens where you left it.

A **Contents** tab lists the PDF's bookmarks, or its section headings when it has none, and marks the current section. Citations and cross-references inside a paper jump to their target, and web and DOI links open in the browser.

Annotation works directly on the page, as in Acrobat. Round tool bubbles float at the bottom of the reader: Select, Highlighter, Underline, Comment, Add text, Pen, Rectangle and a Page menu. Click the page with Comment to type a comment in place and post it, or with Add text to type straight onto the page. Comment pins can be opened to edit or delete. Selecting text shows a bubble bar with Highlight, Underline, Strikethrough, Comment, Quote to Notes and Copy. Drawing and text tools offer floating colour swatches and a size control. Choosing a tool no longer opens the side panel; the document controls share one row with Download and Export.

Multi-column papers are read in the right order. Pulse detects any number of columns, reads small-print side notes after the main text, rejoins justified lines, finds abstracts that have no "Abstract" heading, keeps hanging-indent references whole across columns and page breaks, and leaves repeated running headers, footers and page numbers out of the extracted text. In the reader, selections stay within a column, so multi-line quotations verify.

Clicking a link between papers on the map opens its details in the right-hand panel.

Mac and Windows packages use the same 2.1.6 source and include Python, PDF/OCR tools and the offline search ranker. Existing Pulse 2.1.2 or later installations can use **Settings → App updates**. Update packages carry signatures verified by Pulse. The Mac app remains ad hoc signed and is not Apple-notarized; the Windows installer has no publisher certificate.
